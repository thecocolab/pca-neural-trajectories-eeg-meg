A primer on low-dimensional neural dynamics

PCA-based trajectory analysis for EEG and MEG

An evoked response is usually drawn as one curve per sensor. The sensors don't change independently, though. The whole pattern moves together, and PCA lets you follow that movement as a path through a few dimensions.

This site holds the results that go with our tutorial, on two open datasets. The figures are interactive, so you can rotate them, zoom in, and check the numbers behind them.

Run it yourself

Six notebooks, meant to be read in this order. They use a handful of participants so they finish on a laptop. Each has a script in scripts/ that runs the same analysis on everyone and writes the reports above. Installation notes are in the README.

  1. EEG: the core workflow

    Fit PCA, plot trajectories for executed and imagined hand movements, and measure how they differ.

  2. EEG: PCA next to UMAP, PHATE and Isomap

    The same data through three nonlinear methods, and how to compare them fairly.

  3. EEG: decoding across participants

    Train on some people and test on someone new, from sensors and from aligned components.

  4. MEG: faces in one shared space

    Famous, unfamiliar and scrambled images in a single PCA space, with the two planned contrasts.

  5. MEG: frequency bands

    Trajectories of alpha, beta and low-gamma power instead of the broadband signal.

  6. MEG: decoding across participants

    The decoding workflow again, this time on MEG.

Data: PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery and Wakeman & Henson faces (OpenNeuro ds000117). Code: thecocolab/pca-neural-trajectories-eeg-meg.
